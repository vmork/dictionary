Origin and history of test

test (n.)

late 14c., teste , "small vessel used in assaying precious metals,"from Old French test , from Latin testum "earthen pot "(in Medieval Latin especially "earthen vessel in which metals are tried "); which is related to testa "shell of shellfish,"testudo "tortoise."In Watkins, etc., this is derived from PIE root *teks- "to weave,"also "to fabricate."But de Vaan (2008) writes, "Derivation from the root *tek- 'to build 'is unlikely for semantic reasons,"and "The word testa is probably a loanword, as words for vessels often are."

The sense of "trial or examination to determine the correctness of something "is recorded from 1590s (Nashe, making a figure from the metallurgical sense); hence "any critical trial or examination."The comparison is to ascertaining the quality of a metal by melting it in a pot. By Johnson 's day (1755) test could mean "means of trial "generally.

In mechanics, "action meant to establish the physical properties of materials or constructions "with an eye to durability, safety, etc. (as in crash-test ), by 1877. To test-drive (v.) an automobile is by 1954.

The sense of "procedure for determining and measuring academic accomplishment, native intelligence, etc."is by 1905, "in academic and similar contexts usu. implying a simpler, less formal procedure than an exam "[OED, 1989].

With verb put (put to the test ) by 1778 and this form was common from early 19c.; earlier the phrase was bring to the test (Shakespeare).

In law, test case is by 1841. In reference to nuclear weapons development, test-ban is by 1958. In English history Test Act was given to various laws meant to exclude Catholics and Nonconformists from office (especially that of 1673, repealed 1828) by requiring oaths of supremacy and allegiance, subscription to doctrines, or reception of sacraments obnoxious to their faiths.

also from late 14c.

test (v.)

c. 1600, "assay gold or silver "in a test ; from test (n.) in the original sense. The figurative sense of "examine the correctness of "is by 1748, on the notion of "put to the proof."The meaning "administer a test "(of knowledge, intelligence, health, etc., to a person) is from 1939; the sense of "undergo a test "is from 1934. The meaning "carry out a test "is by 1961. To test the water figuratively is by 1888; early uses suggest the reference is to testing well-water for purity. Related: Tested ; testing .

also from c. 1600

Entries linking to test

pretest

also pre-test , by 1949 as a verb ("to test before ") and noun ("experimental test to assess the questions or methods intended for a projected test "), from pre- "before "+ test .

retest (v.)

also re-test , "test anew or again,"1863, from re- "back, again "+ test (v.). Related: Retested; retesting . As a noun, "a new or repeated test,"by 1887.
